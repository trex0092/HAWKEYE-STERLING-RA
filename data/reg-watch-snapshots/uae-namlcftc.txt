reckoner with precise answers to the most probable queries why is it important to combat illicit financial flows? protecting the integrity of the financial system is essential to maintaining public confidence and international trust. a reputation for integrity is one of the most valuable assets of a financial institution. evidence of complicity in criminal activity can damage the confidence of regulators, partners, and consumers — both locally and internationally. combating illicit financial flows also protects individuals and businesses from fraud, scams, and other financial crimes. unchecked money laundering has far-reaching social and economic consequences. it enables corruption, undermines governance, and weakens public institutions. it can distort markets, increase financial instability, and facilitate criminal infiltration into legitimate sectors of the economy. preventing illicit flows safeguards economic integrity, supports sustainable growth, and helps ensure that the uae remains a trusted global financial hub. what is the financial action task force (fatf)? the financial action task force (fatf) is an intergovernmental organisation based in paris that sets global standards to combat money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing. established in 1989, fatf issues a set of 40 recommendations that form the international benchmark for national aml/cft/cpf systems. each country is assessed through periodic mutual evaluations to measure how effectively it implements these standards. fatf’s framework allows flexibility for countries to adapt measures to their own legal and institutional systems, ensuring consistency while respecting national contexts. the uae works closely with fatf and regional bodies to promote international cooperation and strengthen financial integrity worldwide. how has the uae strengthened its national framework in recent years? following the recommendations of the fatf’s 2020 assessment, the uae has undertaken significant reforms to build a more effective and sustainable aml/cft/cpf system. these include new legislation, enhanced supervision, risk-based regulatory frameworks, and the establishment of permanent coordination mechanisms through the namlcftpfc and its eight sub-committees. the general secretariat has led this transformation, driving national collaboration and supporting measurable progress across all fatf areas. in february 2024, the uae’s progress was recognised internationally when it was successfully removed from fatf’s list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring — an achievement reflecting the country’s deep commitment to integrity, transparency, and international partnership. who we are about us leadership general secretariat committees contact what we do
