# Conflict of Interest & Staff Conduct Policy

**Owner:** MLRO (accountable) · Compliance Officer (operational, with HR)
**Approver:** Board of Directors
**Status:** DRAFT — for Board approval (open-actions item 18)
**Version:** 0.1 · **Review cadence:** annually, and on any change to the ownership, management or business model.

**Regulatory basis:** Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 (internal controls;

## 1. Why a compliance pack contains a conduct policy

An AML/CFT programme fails at the point where someone has a reason to look away.
Ownership overlap with a customer, a personal relationship with a counterparty,
a commission that depends on the deal closing, a gift arriving before a decision
— none of these are exotic. They are the ordinary shape of the pressure the
control framework has to withstand.

## 2. What counts as a conflict

A conflict exists where a personal interest could reasonably be seen to
influence a decision taken for the firm. Actual influence is not required;
**reasonable appearance is enough.** Examples, not a closed list:

- ownership, directorship, employment or a family relationship in a customer,
  supplier, refinery or counterparty;
- a personal or family relationship with a beneficial owner or a PEP the firm
  screens;
- remuneration, commission or a target that depends on onboarding a specific
  customer or completing a specific transaction;
- an outside business activity in the same sector;
- a gift, hospitality or benefit from a party with an interest in a compliance
  decision;
- performing two roles that must stay separate (preparer and approver of the
  same file; the person who screens and the person who clears the hit).

## 3. Rules

1. **Declare on arrival, on change, and annually.** Every employee, officer and
   director declares conflicts on appointment, within five business days of a
   new one arising, and in an annual confirmation.
2. **Withdraw from the decision.** A conflicted person takes no part in the
   assessment, approval, screening disposition, filing decision or pricing of
   the matter concerned. Declaring and then participating is not compliance.
3. **Four eyes on the exposed decisions.** Customer acceptance, screening
   dispositions and filing decisions are never taken and approved by the same
   person. The four-eyes/segregation-of-duties breach is a zero-tolerance item
   in the [risk appetite](../governance/risk-appetite-statement-2026.md).
4. **Gifts and hospitality.** No gift or benefit is accepted from a party with
   an interest in a compliance decision. Anything of more than nominal value is
   declined or declared and recorded; nothing is accepted in cash or precious
   metal.
5. **Outside interests** in the precious-metals trade require prior written
   approval from the MLRO and the Board.
6. **No commercial override of a compliance decision.** A decision to decline,
   escalate, freeze or report is not commercially reversible. Pressure to
   reverse one is itself a reportable matter under
   [`whistleblowing-policy.md`](whistleblowing-policy.md).
7. **Confidentiality.** Customer information, screening results and reporting
   activity are used only for the purpose for which they were collected. The
   tipping-off prohibition (Article 25, Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025)
   applies to every employee, permanently, and survives leaving the firm.
8. **Integrity of records.** No record is backdated, altered after the fact, or
   created to describe a check that was not performed.

## 4. The MLRO's own conflicts

The MLRO declares conflicts to the **Board chair**, not to management. Where a
matter conflicts the MLRO, the decision passes to the Board chair, and the
substitution is recorded. The compliance function carries no commercial target
on the outcome of any decision it advises on — that independence is stated in
[`compliance-governance-charter.md`](compliance-governance-charter.md) and is
what this clause protects.

## 5. Register and records

A conflicts register records, per declaration: who declared, the date, the
nature of the interest, the parties affected, the mitigation applied (recusal,
reassignment, four-eyes, approval), and the approver. Held by the Compliance
Officer, reviewed at least annually, retained five years, and in the scope of
the [independent audit](independent-audit-policy.md).

An empty register over a full year is a finding to investigate, not a clean
bill of health.

## 6. Breach

A breach is a disciplinary matter and may be a criminal offence where it
involves falsified records, tipping-off or facilitation. Breaches are recorded
in the CAPA log with a root cause, and repeated or deliberate breaches are
escalated to the Board.
