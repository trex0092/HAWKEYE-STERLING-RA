# Responsible Sourcing Policy (gold and precious metals)

**Owner:** MLRO (accountable) · Compliance Officer (operational)
**Approver:** Board of Directors
**Status:** DRAFT — for Board approval (open-actions item 18)
**Version:** 0.1 · **Review cadence:** annually, and on any change to LBMA/OECD guidance or the supplier base.

**Regulatory basis:** MoE Circular No. 2 of 2024 (due diligence for responsible
sourcing of gold — five-step framework) · OECD Due Diligence Guidance for
Responsible Supply Chains of Minerals from Conflict-Affected and High-Risk Areas
· LBMA Responsible Gold Guidance · Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 (predicate
offences including those connected to illicit trade).

---

## 1. Position

The firm sources gold and precious metals only where provenance can be
established and documented. Where origin cannot be established, the material is
not accepted — commercial pressure is not a basis for an exception.

## 2. The five-step framework

| Step | What the firm does |
|---|---|
| **1. Management system** | This policy, a named owner, a supplier register, a grievance channel, and record-keeping for five years |
| **2. Identify and assess risk** | Know Your Supplier due diligence on every counterparty; determine country of origin and transport route; assess CAHRA exposure |
| **3. Design and implement a response** | Risk-mitigation plan with senior-management sign-off; enhanced measures or refusal for high-risk supply |
| **4. Independent audit** | Third-party audit of the due-diligence practice at the frequency the applicable guidance requires |
| **5. Report** | Annual responsible-sourcing report covering the above |

## 3. Know Your Supplier (KYS)

Every supplier and counterparty is subject to due diligence equivalent in rigour
to customer CDD: legal identity and licensing; beneficial ownership at the 25%
threshold with nominees looked through; sanctions and PEP screening; adverse
media; country of origin and refinery chain; and, for mined material, the mine
or ASM source and the transport route.

Suppliers are risk-tiered. High-tier suppliers require enhanced measures:
documentary chain of custody, senior-management approval, and shortened review
cycles.

## 4. CAHRA and ASM

Material originating in, or transiting, a **conflict-affected or high-risk area**
is not accepted without the OECD documentation set for that origin. **Artisanal
and small-scale mined (ASM)** gold from a high-risk jurisdiction is treated as an
OECD Annex II red flag and forces enhanced due diligence — the firm's assessment
methodology escalates it automatically.

## 5. Red flags

- Origin documentation that is inconsistent, generic, or cannot be traced to a
  named mine, refinery or licensed dealer.
- Route or consignee changes after contract execution.
- Pricing materially away from market without commercial explanation.
- Material offered in unusual form, or in quantities inconsistent with the
  supplier's stated capacity.
- Supplier resistance to naming the ultimate source or the refinery.
- Cash settlement pressure for material whose provenance is thin.

Any red flag is escalated to the MLRO and assessed for suspicion under
[`transaction-monitoring-reporting-policy.md`](transaction-monitoring-reporting-policy.md);
a sourcing concern and a reporting duty are separate questions.

## 6. Records and reporting

Provenance documentation, KYS files, risk assessments, mitigation decisions,
audit reports and the annual report are retained for at least five years and are
producible within 48 hours of a competent authority's request.

## 7. Current state — stated, not hidden

The firm's responsible-sourcing **programme execution** (supplier files, the
Step 4 independent audit, the annual report) sits with the business and is not
evidenced in this repository — tracked as obligation OB-19. What the repository
does hold is the monitoring of LBMA and OECD sources through Regulatory Watch, so
a change in guidance is detected rather than missed.
