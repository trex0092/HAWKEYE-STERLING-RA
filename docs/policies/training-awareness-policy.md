# Training & Awareness Policy

**Owner:** MLRO (accountable) · Compliance Officer (operational, with HR)
**Approver:** Board of Directors
**Status:** DRAFT — for Board approval (open-actions item 18)
**Version:** 0.1 · **Review cadence:** annually, and on any material regulatory change requiring content updates.

**Regulatory basis:** Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, Article 17 (ongoing
training programme) · Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025 · MoE AML/CFT
Guidelines for DNFBPs (September 2025).

---

## 1. Principle

Every person whose work can touch a customer, a transaction, a supplier or a
compliance output receives AML/CFT/CPF training appropriate to that role, before
they perform it and at least annually thereafter. Training that is delivered but
not recorded cannot be evidenced, and on inspection an unevidenced control is an
absent one.

## 2. Who is trained, and in what

| Audience | Core content | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Board / senior management** | Obligations and personal liability, risk appetite, MLRO independence, the firm's risk profile, enforcement exposure | Annually |
| **MLRO / Compliance** | Full framework: CDD/SDD/EDD, TFS and freezing duties, STR/DPMSR filing, PF typologies, goAML mechanics, tipping-off, record-keeping | Annually + on change |
| **Front office / sales** | Red flags at the counter, CDD collection, threshold awareness (AED 55,000), escalation route, tipping-off prohibition, no-override rule | Annually + induction |
| **Operations / finance** | Payment and wire-transfer rules (AED 3,500 data requirements), third-party payments, structuring patterns, record retention | Annually + induction |
| **Procurement / sourcing** | Responsible sourcing, KYS, CAHRA and ASM red flags, provenance documentation | Annually + induction |
| **All staff (incl. users of AI output)** | What the tools do and do not decide, why a human signs every decision, acceptable use, AI-specific limitations | Annually |

**PF is a named module**, distinct from ML and TF content — proliferation
typologies, dual-use and end-use concerns, and the sanctions-evasion role of
gold. It is not a paragraph inside the sanctions slide.

## 3. Delivery and assessment

Training is delivered in a recordable form (facilitated session or e-learning
with a completion record). Each module carries an **assessment**, with a pass
mark; a failure is retaken, and repeated failure is escalated to the MLRO as a
competence concern rather than logged and forgotten.

Induction training is completed **before** the person performs the relevant work.

## 4. Records

The training record captures, per person: the module, the date, the delivery
method, the assessment result, and the evidence (attendance or completion
record). Records live in
[`../aims/competency-records.md`](../aims/competency-records.md) and are retained
for at least five years.

**Current state — stated, not hidden.** Cadence is scheduled in the compliance
calendar and the record table exists, but delivery beyond the compliance
function and the named record rows are **outstanding** (obligation OB-11,
open-actions item 7).

## 5. Cadence and triggers

Annual refresh for every audience, plus ad-hoc training on: a new or amended
instrument or supervisory circular; a new product, channel or market; a control
failure or audit finding that implicates understanding; and a material change to
the screening or assessment tooling.

Scheduled duties are filed with lead time from `data/compliance-calendar.json`
(annual AI-awareness refresh; TFS and sanctions-evasion refresh).

## 6. Effectiveness

Training is judged by outcomes, not attendance: escalation quality and volume
from the front office, error rates in CDD files sampled by audit, and the
proportion of red flags caught at the counter rather than downstream. Where the
indicators do not move, the content changes — not just the delivery date.
