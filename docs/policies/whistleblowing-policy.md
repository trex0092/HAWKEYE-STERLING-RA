# Whistleblowing & Speak-Up Policy

**Owner:** MLRO (accountable) · Board of Directors (oversight)
**Approver:** Board of Directors
**Status:** DRAFT — for Board approval (open-actions item 18)
**Version:** 0.1 · **Review cadence:** annually.

**Regulatory basis:** Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 (internal controls;
protection of reporting persons) · Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025 · MoE
AML/CFT Guidelines for DNFBPs (September 2025).

---

## 1. Why this exists

Most compliance failures are visible to someone before they are visible to a
regulator. This policy makes it safe, and expected, for that person to say so.

## 2. What to report

Anything that suggests the firm's AML/CFT/CPF obligations are not being met,
including:

- pressure — explicit or implied — to onboard a customer, execute a
  transaction, or **not** file a report;
- a control being bypassed, overridden or disabled;
- CDD, screening or record-keeping performed in name only;
- a suspicion that was escalated and appears to have gone nowhere;
- falsified or backdated records;
- a conflict of interest affecting a compliance decision;
- retaliation against someone who raised a concern.

Reporting a **suspicion about a customer** is different: that goes to the MLRO
through the escalation route in
[`transaction-monitoring-reporting-policy.md`](transaction-monitoring-reporting-policy.md).
This policy covers concerns about the **firm's own conduct or controls**.

## 3. How to report

| Route | When to use it |
|---|---|
| Line manager | Ordinary concerns, no conflict |
| **MLRO — direct** | Any AML/CFT/CPF concern, including one implicating management |
| **Board chair — direct** | Where the concern implicates the MLRO, or where an MLRO report produced no response |

Reports may be made **in writing or verbally**, and **anonymously**. An
anonymous report is assessed on its content; it is not discounted because the
source is unknown.

## 4. Protection

- **No retaliation.** Dismissal, demotion, exclusion, or any detriment in
  response to a good-faith report is a disciplinary offence in itself.
- **Good faith, not correctness.** A concern raised honestly is protected even
  if it turns out to be mistaken. Deliberately false reports are not.
- **Confidentiality.** The reporter's identity is disclosed only where the law
  requires it, and the reporter is told before that happens where it is lawful
  to tell them.
- **Tipping-off still applies.** Nothing in this policy permits disclosure to a
  customer that a report has been or may be made.

## 5. What happens to a report

1. **Acknowledged** promptly where the reporter is identifiable.
2. **Assessed** by the MLRO — or, where the MLRO is implicated, by the Board
   chair — for substance and for whether it also constitutes a suspicion
   requiring an external report.
3. **Investigated** proportionately, with the evidence preserved.
4. **Concluded** with an outcome recorded, a corrective action where one is
   needed (into the CAPA log), and feedback to the reporter so far as
   confidentiality allows.
5. **Reported** in aggregate to the Board in the MLRO annual report: how many
   concerns, of what type, and how they were resolved. Zero reports over a full
   year is itself something the Board should ask about.

## 6. Records

Reports, assessments, investigation notes and outcomes are retained for at least
five years, with access restricted to those who need it.
