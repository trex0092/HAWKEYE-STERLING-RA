# Counter-Proliferation Financing (CPF) Policy

**Owner:** MLRO (accountable) · Compliance Officer (operational)
**Approver:** Board of Directors
**Status:** DRAFT — for Board approval (open-actions item 18)
**Version:** 0.1 · **Review cadence:** annually, and on any change to proliferation-related designations or guidance.

**Regulatory basis:** Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, Article 3(3)
(standalone PF offence) · Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025 (internal controls
must cover AML **and** CFT **and** CPF) · Cabinet Decision No. 74 of 2020 (TFS,
including proliferation-related designations).

---

## 1. Why this is a separate policy

Until Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, proliferation financing was addressed
only through sanctions obligations. It is now a **standalone offence and a
distinct risk pillar**. Treating PF as a sub-case of sanctions screening is no
longer sufficient: it must be separately assessed, separately controlled and
separately trained.

**Proliferation** means the illicit or unauthorised trade in materials, systems,
equipment, components or technology contributing to the production or
development of weapons of mass destruction or their delivery means. **PF** is
the provision of funds or financial services that enable it.

## 2. Why a gold dealer is exposed

Gold is a store of value and a sanctions-evasion instrument. The firm's PF
exposure is therefore real and specific:

| PF risk factor | Relevance to this business |
|---|---|
| Customer in, or connected to, a proliferation-risk jurisdiction (e.g. DPRK, Iran) | Direct exposure |
| Gold procured for industrial or technical use with unclear end-use | Dual-use concern |
| Payment routed through jurisdictions with weak PF controls | Layering of PF proceeds |
| Complex multi-entity transaction with no apparent commercial logic | PF concealment |
| Gold used as collateral for financing in sanctioned sectors | Integration of PF proceeds |
| Unexplained changes to consignee, routing or end-use after contract | Diversion indicator |

## 3. Controls

1. **Risk assessment.** The enterprise risk assessment carries a **separate PF
   section** — not a paragraph inside the ML section. See
   [`risk-assessment-methodology.md`](risk-assessment-methodology.md).
2. **CDD.** PF exposure is assessed for every customer as a distinct question:
   jurisdictional nexus, end-use, and the commercial logic of the structure. A
   PF concern forces EDD.
3. **Screening.** Proliferation-related designations (including UN DPRK and
   Iran measures) are within the screening scope in
   [`sanctions-tfs-policy.md`](sanctions-tfs-policy.md); a match follows the
   same freeze-and-report path with no discretion.
4. **End-use enquiry.** Where gold is bought for stated industrial or technical
   use, the intended end-use and end-user are established and recorded before
   execution. An evasive or shifting answer is escalated, not clarified away.
5. **Training.** PF typologies are a named module in
   [`training-awareness-policy.md`](training-awareness-policy.md), distinct
   from ML and TF content.
6. **Senior management approval.** Senior management personally approves the
   PF-specific internal controls, as it does the AML and CFT controls.

## 4. Escalation

Any suspected PF exposure goes to the MLRO immediately and is never resolved at
the desk. The MLRO assesses in parallel: (a) whether a designation applies —
freeze and report under the TFS path; and (b) whether there are reasonable
grounds to suspect PF — file an STR through goAML. Neither answer substitutes
for the other. Tipping-off prohibitions apply identically.

## 5. Records

PF assessments, end-use enquiries and escalations are retained with the customer
file for at least five years and are producible within 48 hours of a competent
authority's request.
