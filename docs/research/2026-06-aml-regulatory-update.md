# AML/CFT Regulatory Update for UAE Dealers in Precious Metals and Stones

Prepared: 12/06/2026. Audience: the compliance function of Hawkeye Sterling LLC and its group entities (DPMS, UAE).

Method note: prepared from a multi-source web research run with adversarial cross-checking. Several official sites (fatf-gafi.org, uaelegislation.gov.ae, uaefiu.gov.ae, gov.uk) block automated reading, so some points rest on official page titles plus consistent reputable secondary sources; those points are marked. Items where sources conflict are listed in section 5 for confirmation with the supervisor. This is a research summary, not legal advice.

## 1. The new UAE AML law: Federal Decree-Law No. (10) of 2025

- The UAE issued Federal Decree-Law No. (10) of 2025 Regarding Anti-Money Laundering, and Combating the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation Financing. It was published in the Official Gazette on 30/09/2025 and came into force on 14/10/2025. It repeals and replaces Federal Decree-Law No. (20) of 2018 (as amended). Confirmed on the official legislation portal (entry 3314) and the Central Bank Rulebook; dates per White & Case and DLA Piper. [1][2][3][4]
- The law adds a third pillar, countering proliferation financing (CPF), alongside AML and CFT, aligning the UAE framework with FATF standards, and expressly covers virtual assets and offences committed through digital means. [3][5]
- Article 11 of the new law establishes the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) as an operationally independent unit within the Central Bank (note: Article 11 is the FIU article, not the sanctions article; our assessment narrative was corrected accordingly on 12/06/2026). [5][6]
- Article 10 introduces a cross-border disclosure system covering currency, bearer negotiable instruments and precious metals and stones for travellers entering or leaving the UAE, with criminal penalties for non-disclosure or false declaration. Directly relevant to DPMS couriers and hand-carry shipments. [7]
- Penalties (as reported by legal commentaries, pending primary-text verification): base money-laundering offence 1 to 10 years imprisonment plus a fine of at least AED 100,000; aggravated cases up to AED 10,000,000 or twice the value of the property; legal persons face fines of AED 5,000,000 up to AED 100,000,000; breaching the reporting duties of Article 18 carries imprisonment and/or fines of AED 100,000 to 1,000,000. [7][8][9]
- Executive Regulations exist: Cabinet Decision No. (134) of 2025 (official portal entry 3857), reported in force from 14/12/2025, replacing Cabinet Decision No. (10) of 2019, with around 71 articles; it strengthens FIU powers (immediate suspensions up to 10 working days; freezes of suspected criminal funds up to 30 days). [10][11]
- DNFBPs, including Dealers in Precious Metals and Stones, remain a regulated category; the Ministry supervising DNFBPs can impose administrative penalties reported in the range of AED 10,000 to AED 5,000,000 (see section 5, item c). [12]

- February 2026 plenary (FATF Week 09/02/2026 to 13/02/2026, plenary 11/02/2026 to 13/02/2026, Mexico City): Kuwait and Papua New Guinea were ADDED to the grey list (jurisdictions under increased monitoring). NO jurisdiction was removed. Yemen reportedly completed its action plan but stays listed pending an on-site visit. [13][14][15]

## 3. UAE supervision and goAML duties for DPMS

- Supervisor: the Ministry of Economy and Tourism (MoET, formerly Ministry of Economy) supervises DPMS as DNFBPs, runs inspection campaigns and fines violators. In H1 2025 it reported 1,063 violations and fines exceeding AED 42 million across DNFBPs; within that, DPMS accounted for 473 violations and about AED 20 million. [20][21]
- National structure: since 09/2024 the National AML/CFT Committee (NAMLCFTC) and its General Secretariat replaced the former Executive Office of AML/CTF; Federal Decree-Law No. (7) of 2024 created the committee structure. Day-to-day DPMS supervision stayed with MoET. [22][23]
- goAML registration: every DPMS must register on the FIU's goAML portal. Registration is two-stage: first the SACM portal (username plus authenticator key), then registration as a Reporting Entity in goAML. Separately, DNFBPs register with the EOCN systems for targeted financial sanctions notifications. [24][25]
- Report types for a DPMS: STR (suspicious transactions), SAR (suspicious activity, including attempted transactions), DPMSR for cash transactions of AED 55,000 or more with any customer, and wire transfers of AED 55,000 or more with legal persons (per the FIU's DPMS guidance and MoE Circular No. 08/2021), and the targeted-financial-sanctions reports (FFR, renamed in 2025 guidance to CNMR for confirmed matches, and PNMR for partial matches). REAR applies to real estate, not DPMS. [26][27][28]
- Deadlines: STR/SAR have no fixed day-count in the law; they must be filed without delay once suspicion forms. The DPMSR is reported as due within two weeks of the qualifying transaction (consulting sources; confirm with the FIU). Sanctions freezes apply without delay (within 24 hours) of a listing, with the freeze report through goAML within about five days (see section 5, item b). [27][29][30]
- Penalties: failing to register or file is fined under Cabinet Resolution No. (16) of 2021, with reported ranges from AED 50,000 up to AED 1,000,000, and up to AED 5,000,000 in severe cases (see section 5, item a). [31]

## 5. Items to confirm with the supervisor or counsel (sources conflict)

a) Non-registration fine cap under Cabinet Resolution 16/2021: AED 1,000,000 vs AED 5,000,000 per violation (sources differ). [31]
b) Funds-freeze report deadline: five calendar days vs five business days; and the FFR to CNMR renaming under the 2025 TFS guidelines. [29][30]
c) DNFBP administrative fine floor under the new law: AED 10,000 (reported) vs AED 50,000 (old regime). [12]
d) The exact article of Decree-Law 10/2025 carrying the targeted-financial-sanctions obligations (Article 11 is the FIU article; the TFS article number was not verifiable). [5][6]
