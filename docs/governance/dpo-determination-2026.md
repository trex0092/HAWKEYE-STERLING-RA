## 1. The test

PDPL (FDL 45/2021 Art. 10) requires a Data Protection Officer where the
controller/processor's core activity involves (a) processing that would cause
a **high risk** to personal-data privacy and security given its nature, scope
or purposes, (b) **systematic and comprehensive assessment** of sensitive
personal data including profiling/automated decisions, or (c) processing a
**large volume of sensitive** personal data.

## 2. Assessment against this estate's processing

| Trigger | This estate | Read |
|---|---|---|
| High-risk processing by nature/scope | AML/CFT screening of customers and UBOs is regulated, adverse-consequence processing — but deliberately minimised (name + headline to the LLM; identity tokenised on delivery; no biometric/health data) and human-decided at every operative point | Arguable, not clear-cut |
| Systematic evaluation incl. automated decisions | Screening is systematic, but there is **no automated decision**: every disposition is an MLRO act (one-way override, dual attestation) | Not met as automated decision-making |
| Large-scale sensitive data | The book is hundreds of subjects, not mass-market scale; special-category data is not collected by design | Not met |

## 3. Options for the Board (recommendation first)

1. **(Recommended) Determine "not mandatorily required", and designate a
   named privacy-responsible role anyway.** The Art. 10 triggers are not
   squarely met, but the firm processes regulated personal data daily; naming
   an accountable privacy role costs little and evidences accountability to
   the Data Office and examiners. **Independence note:** the MLRO already owns
   most privacy artifacts (DPIA, ROPA, retention); if the role lands on the
   MLRO, the minute should record the dual-hat and the conflict check
   (the privacy role must be able to escalate about the compliance function,
   not only from it). A separate officer or external counsel retainer avoids
   that tension when headcount allows.
2. **Designate a formal DPO now.** Strongest signal, exceeds the legal
   minimum; carries the independence staffing question immediately.
3. **Determine "not required", designate nobody.** Legally defensible on the
   §2 reading, but leaves privacy accountability diffuse; not recommended
   while the estate is expanding.
